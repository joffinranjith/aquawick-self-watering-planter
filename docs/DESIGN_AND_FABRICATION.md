# 🔧 Design & Fabrication Notes

Fabrication steps, the physics behind the wick, and maintenance notes for the **AquaWick** self-watering planter.

[← Back to README](../README.md)

## 1. Fabrication Procedure

### Tools Required

- Utility knife or sharp scissors
- Marker (to mark the cutting line)

> [!CAUTION]
> Cut slowly and away from your body, and smooth any sharp edges left on the plastic.

### Assembly Steps

1. **Clean and prepare:** Rinse a discarded plastic bottle thoroughly. Remove the label and the cap so the neck stays open.
2. **Cut the bottle:** Mark a line around the bottle, around its middle, and cut cleanly into two pieces — a **top half** (with the neck) and a **bottom half**.
3. **Prepare the wick:** Cut a length of natural cotton rope long enough to reach from the root zone of the soil down into the water. Soak it in water for a few minutes so it starts drawing water quickly.
4. **Insert the wick:** Turn the top half upside down (neck facing downward) and thread the cotton rope through the neck, so one end extends up into the soil chamber and the other hangs down towards the reservoir.
5. **Add soil and plant:** Fill the inverted top half with soil while holding the upper end of the wick near the centre of the root zone. Plant the sapling and gently firm the soil.
6. **Fill and assemble:** Fill the bottom half with water, leaving a small gap between the water surface and the bottle neck. Seat the top half onto the bottom half so the wick dips into the water.

## 2. Physics of Capillary Irrigation

Water climbs through the cotton wick by **capillary rise**. For an idealised narrow tube, the height of the rise is given by **Jurin's law**:

$$h = \frac{2 \gamma \cos\theta}{\rho g r}$$

where:

- $h$ = height of the liquid rise
- $\gamma$ = surface tension of water ($\approx 0.0728\ \text{N/m}$ at $20^\circ\text{C}$)
- $\theta$ = contact angle between the water and the cotton fibres
- $\rho$ = density of water ($\approx 1000\ \text{kg/m}^3$)
- $g$ = acceleration due to gravity ($9.81\ \text{m/s}^2$)
- $r$ = effective radius of the pores between the fibres

Cotton fibres are hydrophilic (a small contact angle, so $\cos\theta$ is close to 1) and the gaps between twisted fibres are very narrow (a small $r$). Both effects increase $h$, so water is drawn up the wick against gravity into the drier soil above.

> [!NOTE]
> Jurin's law describes an idealised capillary tube. A real cotton rope is a porous bundle of fibres, so the formula explains the trend rather than predicting the exact flow.

### Self-regulation

The wick moves water only while the soil is drier than the wick. As the soil becomes moist, the difference shrinks and the flow slows down — so the plant is supplied on demand.

## 3. Operation & Maintenance Notes

- **Initial priming:** Keep the wick damp when assembling. A completely dry wick takes longer to start drawing water. Watering the soil lightly from the top once also helps.
- **Air gap:** Leave a small gap between the water surface and the bottle neck, so the neck does not sit in standing water and the roots get air.
- **Refilling:** Top up the bottom reservoir whenever the water runs low. How often depends on temperature, sunlight and the plant.
- **Algae prevention:** If the planter sits in strong sunlight for long periods, covering or painting the reservoir reduces light reaching the water and helps prevent algae.
